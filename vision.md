## Book Concept Summary

This book teaches **applied statistical inference, and the judgment to choose
between paradigms,** to **undergraduates and self-learners who know only the
frequentist toolkit,** by **solving every problem frequentist-first then
Bayesian, side by side, with LLM-generated implementation throughout,**
filling the gap left by **single-paradigm intro-statistics texts and
Bayesian-only books that never teach the choice.** It succeeds if the reader
can **take a real dataset, implement both a frequentist and a Bayesian
analysis of the same question, articulate what each assumes and where each
fails, and make a defensible choice between them.**

## Central Argument

This book argues that **neither the frequentist nor the Bayesian paradigm is
universally correct**, which means that **competent practice is choosing the
right approach for the problem, the data, the decision, and the audience —
after seeing both solved on the same problem**, and this matters because
**defaulting to one paradigm hides the questions it cannot answer and produces
confident, wrong conclusions** (the p-value/posterior confusion, the
replication crisis, the winner's curse).
